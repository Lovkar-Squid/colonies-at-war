**The war itself.**

Until now `/war declare` changed a word and wrote a log line. Nothing marched. 0.2.0 is the warband: the War Room's guards go out, a battle is fought, and they come home with plunder or wounds.

## What's new

- **`/war march <colony> [guards]`.** The War Room's guards leave the world the way the Nether Worker does and walk the map at a minute per 200 blocks, three minutes at the least. A colony whose army is away is softer, and Colonist Thieves' assessment says so.
- **A raid at the gate, when somebody is there to see it.** If the town they march on is being played, the warband arrives as a real MineColonies raid: it stands in the defender's Town Hall beside any barbarian horde, raises the same boss bar ("Warband of Ironhold — from the north"), and is fought by the same guards. Its soldiers are raider mobs statted from the **attacking** colony's men — never from the defender's raid level — and carry the attacker's name: "Soldier of Ironhold", "Captain of Ironhold". Half of them still standing when the siege's time is up — the next nightfall, by default — is a town overrun; fewer is a defence that held.
- **A number, when nobody is.** Strength against strength with luck the size of the fight — the same figures the Hideout already shows as "an army against 400". The report says what was rolled: `overran the town (33 +2 against 80)`.
- **The war goal means something.** Declared for **plunder**, a victory rolls a loot table by the town's wealth and the margin of the win, and the goods are in the warehouse when the men are home (the War Room, then its doorstep, if the warehouse is full). Declared to **raze**, it knocks a breach in the wall nearest the road the army came by — only with `wallsCanBreak`, and the Builder repairs it with `/war raise`.
- **Defeat hurts.** The men come home at a third of their health. A recalled warband comes home unhurt: it never fought.
- **Supply.** Mustering takes two rations a man out of the warehouse. Short of food the men still go, hungry, at three quarters strength, and the report says so.
- **Weariness.** Every campaign adds weariness to both towns — 15 to the one that marched, 10 to the one attacked. It cools three a day; above 60 the War Room refuses ("the men will not march again so soon"); peace clears it.
- **`/war recall`** turns a warband round while it is still on the road. On the field it cannot: the men are fighting.
- **`/war campaigns`** — weariness and every warband out or lately home, with minutes left; **`/war log [lines]`** — the war log, who marched with how many, what happened, what was taken. Both towns' managers get a chat line at each turn of a campaign.
- **Kingdoms.** With The Waking World installed a warband can march on one of its kingdoms — `/war march at <x> <z>` on whatever stands there, `/war targets` to see what does. No declaration, a kingdom is nobody's; the fight is a number against the keep's garrison, engines and arcs of wall as the kingdom keeps them. Win or lose the kingdom hates the colony's owner afterwards — its guards turn on him, its traders refuse him — and its standing falls; a sack costs twice as much and carries off the keep's gold.
- **And the kingdom strikes back.** Some days after an attack its men come to your walls: a real raid, fought by your guards, the next time you are home — more of them and sooner after a sack, forgotten if you stay away a week. If they have their way they carry goods out of the warehouse and break the wall. `/war campaigns` says when they are due.
- **Camps and villages.** A warband can march on what is nobody's: a raider camp — MineColonies' barbarian, amazon and desert camps, the pirate ship, a pillager outpost — or a village. A camp overrun is **cleared**, its spawners smashed for good, and yields the raiders' goods; a village only loses what the men carry off. What counts as which is two structure tags a datapack can extend.
- **Veterans.** Every guard who fought earns experience on his guard job's skills when he is home, half again for a victory, so a warband that has been out is worth more.
- **The men who fell are held, not dead.** After a lost fight the colony or kingdom that beat you keeps up to half the warband until a ransom is paid — `/war ransom`, gold a head out of the warehouse, and a colony that holds them gets the gold — or lets them go after a few days. `/war campaigns` lists who is held and what he costs.
- **Find the Raiders.** A research in the Combat tree, under *A Standing Army*, that gives a town an alarm: every three seconds of a raid, every guard building on patrol is pointed at the raider nearest it, and every idle or napping guard with a raider within reach is set on him at once — no more towers that never notice a raid coming in from the far side of town. Guards told to stand somewhere keep their ground, except against a raider who has got behind them, nearer the Town Hall than their post: that one they go after, and then they go back. Following guards stay with you, a rallying banner still wins. Works on every raid, a neighbour's warband and a kingdom's reprisal included, and beside Colonist Errands' defensive line: the line stands, what slips through is hunted. Off switch: `findTheRaiders`.
- **The Explorer, and expeditions.** A new War Room profession: the one person there who goes out to look rather than to fight. Send him on an expedition — a kind of country (forest, jungle, taiga, desert, savanna, swamp, snow, mountains, ocean, beach, river, badlands, mushroom fields, cherry grove, caves) or an exact biome, a difficulty (easy to deadly), and how many guards go along — and he comes home with the land's goods (a loot table per kind of country, in the datapack) and the drops of the beasts they killed there, rolled on the creatures' own loot tables from the biome's real spawn lists: *killed 3 Zombies, 2 Spiders, 4 Cows*. Harder trips bring more and risk more; driven off, they drop half and come home hurt; everyone learns. The country round the colony is read off the world's noise, nothing is loaded to ask. `/war biomes`, `/war explore`, and the table below.
- **Hue and Cry.** The second bell, a research under *Find the Raiders*: posted guards leave their posts for any raider within forty-eight blocks, not only for one that has got behind them, and go back when he is dead.
- **Peace is a negotiation.** `/war peace` no longer ends a war by itself. One side offers terms — a **white** peace, **tribute** (gold from the other side), **pay** (gold to them), or their **vassalage** — and the other accepts or refuses, by hand, within `offerDays`. Accepting does everything at once: the gold changes warehouses (refused if the payer is short), every man held on either side goes home — a prisoner exchange — a truce holds for `truceDays`, and the war weariness on both sides is gone. `/war offer <colony> [white|tribute <gold>|pay <gold>|vassal]`, `/war accept`, `/war refuse`; or *Peace* and *Accept* on the table.
- **Alliances.** Standing at or above `allyThreshold` (40 — the mirror of the war threshold) unlocks the offer of an alliance the way low standing unlocks a declaration; the other side accepts. Allies cannot declare on each other and convoys between them carry three times the goods. Walking out (`/war break`) costs the standing it stood on. `/war ally`, `/war accept`, `/war break`; or *Ally* on the table.
- **Convoys.** Goods to another colony or a Waking World kingdom, with an escort of War Room guards and a courier from the warehouse when one is free — and the goods are **chosen and brought to the War Room first**, not grabbed off the warehouse's shelves. The War Room's new **convoy tab** holds a manifest: put items on it (a stack a press, out of a search over everything there is or the usual goods), and the room asks the colony for them the way a hut asks for its minimum stock — a courier carries them from the warehouse into the War Room's racks, or you carry them yourself; the tab says what has come, and the couriers' own pick-ups leave it alone. *Convoy* beside a neighbour on the war table then sends what has arrived — the manifest and nothing else, so the knights' swords in the same racks stay — up to `convoyStacks` stacks, three times that to an ally; with nothing loaded the button opens the bay instead. Arriving, the goods go into the partner's warehouse and `convoyStanding` is earned with them — the one way standing rises that costs something, takes time and could be interfered with. At most `convoysPerWeek` to the same partner; no road while at war. To a kingdom it is a gift that also pays a debt: a reprisal it owed you and had not yet sent is forgiven. `/war load <item> [count]`, `/war unload [item] [count]`, `/war convoy <colony> [guards]`, `/war convoy at <x> <z> [guards]`; or the convoy tab and *Convoy* on the table.
- **Sellswords.** At siege depth or deeper the War Room hires mercenaries — `mercenaryGold` a man out of the warehouse, up to `mercenariesPerLevel` times the War Room's level — who wait `mercenaryDays` for the next warband and go with it: six strength a man, and in a raid fought in the world they stand on the field beside the guards. Not citizens: they cannot be held captive, and they take a share of the plunder, a stack for every two of them. `/war hire [count]`, or *Hire* on the table.
- **`warLevel` means something now.** It was in the config from the first cut and did nothing. **skirmish**: a warband marches and a battle is fought, the winner carries off plunder, but no wall is breached, nobody is taken, no sellswords are for hire, and peace is a white peace or gold — the goals *raze* and *conquer* are refused. **siege** (now the default — it is what every earlier cut did regardless): breaches, captives and ransom, sellswords. **conquest**: all of that and the goal *conquer* — a town overrun becomes the victor's **vassal** for `vassalDays`: the war is over at once, the vassal pays `tributeStacks` of goods out of its warehouse to its lord every day, cannot march on him, and is its own again when the days are up or its lord releases it (`/war release`). Vassalage can also be a term of peace. Nobody's colony changes hands. A world whose config already says `skirmish` keeps it and now gets a real skirmish; change it to `siege` to keep what you had.
- **The table.** Three new tabs in the War Room window: **the war table** — every target with distance, direction, strength and standing, *March* lit only when the men could go and the reason in small print when not, a second button for whatever else there is to do with that neighbour (*Peace*, *Accept*, *Withdraw*, *Ally*, *Convoy*), how many go, *Recall*, *Ransom*, *Hire*, the warband out, the men held or the sellswords waiting, the war log — **Expeditions**, with the Explorer, every kind of country within reach, difficulty, escort, and *Go*; and **the convoy**, the manifest and the shelf to fill it from. None of this needs the chat any more.
- **Help.** The red **?** every MineColonies hut has, on the War Room and the Wall Tower too — the same book, nine pages for the room (the table, war, peace, convoys, sellswords, expeditions, the walls, depth) and three for the tower — on the hut tab where MineColonies puts it and in the corner of every tab of ours.
- Twenty-four new server settings under `campaigns`: `siegeInWorld`, `armyLook` (barbarian, norsemen, pirate or amazon), `siegeNights` (1–3), `marchMinutesPer200Blocks`, `marchMinimumMinutes`, `plunderRolls`, `rationsPerGuard`, `marchOnKingdoms`, `kingdomStandingCost`, `kingdomsStrikeBack`, `reprisalDays`, `marchOnCamps`, `campaignExperience`, `captives`, `ransomGold`, `ransomDays`, `truceDays`, `offerDays`, `mercenaryGold`, `mercenariesPerLevel`, `mercenaryDays`, `vassalDays`, `tributeStacks`, `allyThreshold`; four under `expeditions`: `expeditions`, `expeditionRange`, `exploreMinutesPerLevel`, `expeditionRolls`; four under `convoys`: `convoys`, `convoyStacks`, `convoyStanding`, `convoysPerWeek`; and `findTheRaiders` under `war`.

## Fixed

- **The War Room and the Wall Tower held half the garrison they promised.** MineColonies' hiring limit on a guard building is combined: every citizen of every kind counts against each module's maximum. Splitting the garrison between the knight and ranger modules halved it — a level-3 War Room held three men, not six. Both modules now say the whole number, and the mix is yours.
- The inner-corner wall piece had no name in the War Room's wall panel.
- The alarm now leaves a moved post alone when somebody else has given the building orders since — the player, or Colonist Errands standing its line down — and moves a post without a rally outside the colony's own ground, where MineColonies would clear the rally anyway.
- The war table's header keeps up with the server (the warband out, the men at home, the buttons) without closing and reopening the window; place names no longer run off the row; the war log wraps.

## What it took, and what it proved

The warband is a MineColonies colony event, which is what lets a barbarian horde and a neighbour's army stand in the same Town Hall and be fought by the same guards. Two things about that were found in MineColonies' own code before they could bite:

- MineColonies saves a colony event by path alone and reads it back under its own namespace, so an event registered as `colonies_at_war:army` vanished on every reload. It is registered as `minecolonies:caw_army`, and a siege now survives quitting to the title screen.
- A kingdom's reprisal at a real, played town could fail to find a way in — MineColonies' spawn-point search walks the countryside and gives up when a chunk is not there, and the reprisal asked it only once. It now asks ten times, as MineColonies' own raids do, and the men come.
- MineColonies' own raid bookkeeping reads a history line its raids write and ours never did — once out of every soldier's AI the moment the camp is struck (a warband that stood at its fires all night), once when the raid ended. The warband now writes itself the same line, which is also the honest thing: the Town Hall's raid info lists it, its dead are counted, and citizens lost to it weigh on the town's raid difficulty like any other raid.

All of it was run headless before it reached you: two colonies, six guards, seven campaigns — a number; a raid saved and loaded while it stood, six soldiers on the field 140 blocks out who struck camp and walked on the town, four of them cut down and the night decided as a defence that held; a breach; a recall; a second raid that overran the town; peace made behind the warband's back; a siege left standing in a town that went quiet — men home visible, hurt or laden each time, plunder in the warehouse, the log and `/war campaigns` right after a restart, a server stopped and started with the warband on the road, against The Waking World a kingdom sacked and its eleven men at Ironhold's gate a day later, a barbarian camp found a thousand blocks out and cleared of its five spawners, a village robbed, men held after two lost fights and let go or ransomed; for the alarm, a raid called down a hundred and sixty blocks from four towers whose knights never turned toward it until the research was finished, and then did, and a sentry who left his post for the one raider that got behind it and went back when he was dead; and for the Explorer, the country surveyed, a normal trip to the snow with two knights and a deadly one alone into the wilds, the haul in the warehouse, the expedition saved and loaded on the road; and for the terms, three towns at conquest depth — two sellswords hired and refused past the cap and the purse, a war to conquer won with them, the loser a vassal paying tribute out of its warehouse into the victor's when a day passed and freed when the days were up, peace for ten gold offered, refused as unpayable at a hundred, and accepted with a truce and no weariness, a white peace refused, an alliance offered and accepted with neither able to declare, a manifest loaded, capped, asked for from the couriers and brought, a stack taken off it under the cap and the rest left, a convoy that carried the manifest and nothing else out of the room and never touched the warehouse, delivered with the standing up by eight, the weekly cap, the road closed at war, the alliance broken. Then in the game itself: the research finished in the University's tree, the alarm raised against a barbarian raid at the shore, the Explorer hired from the War Room, an expedition to the Forest sent from the table and home with its haul, a barbarian raid beside Colonist Errands' line, a sellsword hired, and a convoy loaded on the tab, asked for from the couriers, its goods counted in the War Room's racks, and sent from the table. Zero warnings from the mod.
